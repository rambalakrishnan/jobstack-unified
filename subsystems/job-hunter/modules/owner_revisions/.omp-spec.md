## Invariants & Test Assertions
### Invariants
- **[OWD-OWNER]**: Every declared domain has one authenticated logical writer. Entity registration fixes its field set, authority source, writer, storage-domain identity, and publication policy. Temporary workers act through that writer's fenced transition gateway; they do not become additional owners. Each owner_domain is distinct within an entity.
- **[OWD-LOCAL-COMMIT]**: Commit an owner's authorized effect, revision advance, and journal evidence atomically within that owner's durability boundary. Do not acknowledge before durability. No owner commit acquires a peer-owned write lock or waits for a peer transaction, acknowledgement, or large-payload serialization.
- **[OWD-REVISION]**: A domain revision is a complete immutable snapshot, not a patch. revision=previous_revision+1 at the owner. The first revision is 1. The key (entity, domain, revision) identifies exactly one content digest. Identical retries are idempotent; different bytes under that key are an integrity conflict. Stale expected revisions or lease epochs cannot commit.
- **[OWD-ATTEMPT]**: Attempt slots contain the exact lifecycle AxisRevision publications. Their outer slot revision equals the embedded axis revision; the axis, attempt identity, commission digest, owner_domain, and writer_id match registration and commission. The reconciler writes only report.
- **[LIFE-AXES]**: Exactly one authority role owns each of the five axes. Each applicable axis has exactly one bound publication writer. A writer may publish only its axis and must preserve the identity of its authoritative source. A report writer must not hold execution/application write authority. No whole-attempt status or multi-axis update command is defined here.
- **[LIFE-PUBLICATION]**: Axis revisions are complete immutable publications. The key is (attempt_id, kind, axis, revision); a different value under the same key is an integrity conflict. Each next revision is previous_revision + 1. Only the owner-local revision check and journal commit are atomic. Publication to readers is asynchronous and cannot require another owner's transaction, acknowledgement, or payload serialization.
- **[LIFE-CLAIMS]**: Execution publications contain only faithfully mapped, source-attributed claims from the commissioned subject/version. Preserve raw responses, mapping versions, declared scope, original times when available, and receipt times. An absent state is Missing, not an invented subject claim of unknown. Explicit unknown remains a valid subject claim.
- **[LIFE-ORIGIN]**: An application fact must identify the commissioned origin, candidate, job, and attempt, and cite verified source and correlation evidence under the pinned origin policy. A local fixture fact cites its transaction; a production fact cites an actual verified origin receipt. HTTP 200, a submit click, a subject result, or an operator assertion alone is insufficient.
- **[LIFE-CLOSE]**: Active observation, transport connection, native execution, application mutation authority, and native context lifetime are distinct. On the local active deadline or end request, stop issuing workflow commands, record native-cancel availability and any attempt, record transport closure, and drain effects/evidence only within the commissioned bound.
- **[EK-ATOMICITY]**: A locally authoritative operation commits its admitted input, idempotency record, authoritative effects, journal events, and replayable response as one owner-local durable effect before acknowledgement. Install referenced artifact bytes durably before that commit. Cross-owner publication remains asynchronous; this rule does not authorize a shared transaction across owners.
- **[EK-LEASE]**: Claim atomically checks eligible resource state and absence of an unexpired lease, assigns a unique fencing lease_id and owner epoch, advances the entity revision, clears last_failure_reason, and appends evidence without changing resource_state. Completion checks lease ID, unexpired authority-clock deadline, entity/input revisions, and applicable owner/session epochs. A rejected stale completion has no effect.

## 15. Publication, archives, retention, and leases

### 15.1 Publication boundary

Owner durability and read-view visibility are separate events.

A `W.PublicationReceipt` contains:

`record_type`, `schema_version`, `receipt_id`, `entity`, `registration_digest`, `view_id`, `publisher_id`, `domain`, `revision`, `document`, `supersedes`, `observed_owner_head`, `source_prefix`, `installed_at`, `publication_ref`, and `publication_delay`.

Complete bytes are transferred, validated, and installed before pointer advance. Failure leaves the prior complete revision visible. Reordered deliveries cannot roll back the current pointer. Identical retries return the existing receipt.

Publication delay is a valid kernel measurement, not arbitrary source/destination UTC subtraction.

### 15.2 Seals

`W.SealPoint` contains `seal_id`, `name`, `view_id`, `versions`, `sealed_at`, `input_prefix`, and `evidence_refs`.

A seal pins locally available complete publications. It does not require global synchronous freshness or freeze future writes.

Job seals use `published`, `fetch_key_replacement`, or `evidence_updated`. Attempt seals use `commissioned`, `observation_closed`, or `evidence_updated`.

### 15.3 Crawler replacement obligations observed by Job Hunter

Job Hunter never changes acquisition to force a refresh.

- Same fetch key means acquisition no-op, even if a newer response or extractor exists.
- Independent classification/lease/discovery changes may advance the crawler revision.
- Changed fetch key requires a complete prior-row archive.
- The replacement cut includes the old crawler revision and latest fully published downstream revision in the named ingest-side view.
- Unpublished product updates are excluded.
- A concurrent checked-pointer change requires reselection.
- Product publication must make complete immutable downstream bytes available before installation.
- Crawler replacement must not wait for peer transactions or serialize large downstream payloads under its writer lock.

### 15.4 Archive and retention

`W.ArchiveRecord` contains `record_type`, `schema_version`, `root`, `storage_manifest`, and complete `payload_json`.

The root hashes the canonical storage manifest, not the expanded wrapper. The manifest contains `encoding`, `schema_version`, `entity`, `registration`, `domains`, and `seal_point`.

Expansion reconstructs the exact complete old logical row without consulting mutable peer state. Hash lists or crawler-only snapshots are insufficient.

`FetchKeyReplacement` retains `replacement_id`, entity and registration identity, old crawler pin/key, complete new crawler revision, archive root, archived vector, retention pin ID, commit reference, prefix, and evidence references.

`RetentionPinRevision` retains pin/owner/writer identity, revision pair, dependent, archive root, state, release reference, prefix, and support. State is active or released. Release requires evidenced retirement; transitive roots remain protected.

Loss is an integrity incident, not successful garbage collection.

### 15.5 Product leases

Product work uses kernel leases independently of crawler leases.

The lease record retains resource, policy, issuer, authority epoch, entity/input revisions, generation, resource and eligible state, lease state, token, owner, owner/session epochs, issue/expiry points, retries, failure reason, prefix, and evidence.

Claiming does not change resource state. Completion checks current token, unexpired authority-clock deadline, revisions, and epochs. Stale completion has no effect. Failed generations increment retries at most once.

Authority restart durably fences the old epoch. Lease expiry revokes local commit authority, not evidence that native work or employer transactions stopped.
