## Invariants & Test Assertions
### Invariants
- **[EK-LEASE]**: Claim atomically checks eligible resource state and absence of an unexpired lease, assigns a unique fencing lease_id and owner epoch, advances the entity revision, clears last_failure_reason, and appends evidence without changing resource_state. Completion checks lease ID, unexpired authority-clock deadline, entity/input revisions, and applicable owner/session epochs. A rejected stale completion has no effect.
- **[EK-LEASE-RESTART]**: leased_at and lease_expires_at use one registered lease-authority clock and boot; expiry must be later than issue. Renewal requires a still-current token and epoch. On authority restart, durably fence the old authority epoch before reclaiming its leases. Never compare old offsets with the new boot, and never infer elapsed downtime from UTC.
- **[EK-ATOMICITY]**: A locally authoritative operation commits its admitted input, idempotency record, authoritative effects, journal events, and replayable response as one owner-local durable effect before acknowledgement. Install referenced artifact bytes durably before that commit. Cross-owner publication remains asynchronous; this rule does not authorize a shared transaction across owners.
- **[OWD-REVISION]**: A domain revision is a complete immutable snapshot, not a patch. revision=previous_revision+1 at the owner. The first revision is 1. The key (entity, domain, revision) identifies exactly one content digest. Identical retries are idempotent; different bytes under that key are an integrity conflict. Stale expected revisions or lease epochs cannot commit.
- **[OWD-LOCAL-COMMIT]**: Commit an owner's authorized effect, revision advance, and journal evidence atomically within that owner's durability boundary. Do not acknowledge before durability. No owner commit acquires a peer-owned write lock or waits for a peer transaction, acknowledgement, or large-payload serialization.

### 16.6 Lease behavior

Claim checks eligible pending state and no unexpired lease, assigns unique token and epochs, advances revision, clears failure, appends evidence, and preserves state.

Completion requires current token, unexpired authority-clock deadline, compatible entity/input revision, current worker/session epochs, and allowed transition. Stale completion has no entity effect.

Renewal requires the current held generation and cannot resurrect an expired lease.

Early failure release and expiry/death recovery clear ownership and increment retry once. Remaining retries preserve pending state. Exhaustion applies terminal routing and a dead-letter occurrence using the recorded reason or `retry_exhausted`.

Successful completion is not a failed retry.

Authority restart fences its old epoch before reclaiming. Old monotonic offsets are not compared with a new boot. Worker-death fencing revokes commit authority; it is not evidence that remote execution stopped.

### 21.4 Recovery

- Acknowledged entities, results, cursor boundaries, and audit survive the stated durable-storage fault model.
- Restart does not refetch proven partitions.
- Incomplete packets/walks may replay idempotently.
- Confirmed worker death fences ownership and reclaims within admitted lease bounds.
- Source A4’s 3-second detection/10-second replacement and A5’s 100-ms skew are assumptions, not shared guarantees.
- Storage failure causes no acknowledgement or cursor advance.
- Session rejection is detected before accepting content.
- One target’s challenge, handoff, or model pressure cannot block siblings.
- Unknown lifetime does not justify invented keepalive requests.
